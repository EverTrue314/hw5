Reflection

Thoughts on the agent runs, their results, and possible future uses.

Overall, I think the agents’ performance on the tickets was varied. Ticket 102 was the most successful: the Boss called the two appropriate agents, and Accounting solved its initial lookup problem by finding the correct lease record. Facilities and Accounting confirmed the $2,400 rent amount and due date for human review. Tickets 101 and 103 were also relatively successful, with a few missing pieces. For ticket 101, the Boss called two of the three agents I expected. Inventory and Accounting identified the $840 invoice and its vendor context, and a human later approved the payment. Customer service was not called, and the database still does not establish the tee order quantity or delivery. For ticket 103, Inventory correctly found that 8 of 20 requested hoodies were in stock, and Accounting found the $22 unit cost. Customer service was not called to draft a response, and the unspecified discount and sourcing decision remain open.

For all three runs, the actual workflow differed from my plan. Customer service was not called for tickets 101 or 103, so no email drafts were generated. On ticket 101, Inventory used the additional get_ticket_invoice_vendor_context tool, which helped connect the reprint invoice with Bulldog Print Co. On ticket 102, Accounting used get_lease_details rather than the expected accounting snapshot, and its first lease ID query returned no record before it checked the linked lease. The final run established the rent amount and due date, but payment was made afterward by a human. Ticket 103 had the largest delegation difference because Customer service was not called. The backend marked its agent workflow resolved and correctly recorded no cash change, although price and sourcing decisions still need follow-up.

The earlier attempts used gpt-5.6-luna because the project-level setup instructions specified that model, and the agent configuration followed those instructions. I later checked the Homework 5 requirement and updated the agents to gpt-6-luna. The append-only audit trail preserves the earlier attempts, while the Actual sections, cash figures, and this reflection describe the successful final runs using gpt-6-luna.

I think ticket 103 could be handled more efficiently by one agent with tools, since the run involved no payment or purchase approval. One agent could review the ticket, flag the inventory shortfall, identify that the requested price is missing, and prepare an email draft for review. The actual run involved three agents total (the Boss, Inventory, and Accounting), compared with the four agents I expected when I included Customer service. The separate agents provided useful inventory and accounting findings, but the missing customer-service draft left out a customer-facing next step.

The agents could provide a different product suggestion if the one requested is sold out. The agents could generate reports on what items are most popular for group or custom orders. The agents could suggest similar items that are cheaper for groups seeking discounts and include that list in a customer service agent generated email draft.

The agents couldn’t track an order or shipment, so it wouldn’t be able to provide an update to customers on potential restock dates. They also couldn’t predict what products may go out of stock faster and recommend reordering in advance of inventory running out. And finally, it couldn’t compare availability across suppliers for specific item restocks, so it couldn’t help campus customs find the best deals for restocking a specific item.
