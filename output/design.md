# Agent dashboard design

## Chosen look

The dashboard uses the Homework 4 Campus Customs visual style: Yale navy, warm white, soft stone, and a small amount of gold, with Playfair Display headings and DM Sans for interface text. It reuses the Homework 4 Bulldog line-art mark and pairs it with original shirt illustrations labeled for the Boss, Inventory, Accounting, Facilities, and Customer service roles. Each role has its own shirt and outline colors: navy and gold for Boss, green for Inventory, burgundy for Accounting, teal for Facilities, and purple for Customer service. The team panel is labeled “Team roster” and “Meet the pack.”

## Why this fits

The established palette and Bulldog mark make the dashboard feel like part of the same Campus Customs project. The warm background and editorial headings keep it friendly, while distinct role colors make each agent easy to identify. Clear status colors, compact tool labels, and strong action buttons make live work and human approvals easy to scan.

## Layout and interaction

The three ticket cards sit at the top, show the full ticket subject on as many lines as needed, and put status and approval badges below the subject. They expand when selected to show operational details, a summary, and any pending approval. A shield badge on a ticket marks a payment or purchase that has been recorded with a human approver; the expanded details show the action, amount, approver, and date so earlier agent statements such as “unpaid invoice” are understood as run-time findings. Approval cards prominently show the requested amount. Invoice payment details come from the current invoice record; rent payment details come from the ticket's linked lease, including monthly rent, property, landlord, and due date. The rent amount is checked against the lease and available cash before a human-approved payment can be recorded. Invoice cards also show status, due date, description, and linked supplier details. The supplier is labeled as the invoice vendor, with a note when the database does not establish a SKU restock relationship. The team diagram places Boss above the four specialists and marks peer delegation links; agent cards show concise role, status, and tools without repeating their full statements. When live parent/child run events show Boss delegating, a tennis ball animates from Boss to the specialist and back when the specialist completes its work. The live activity list provides agent statements and the sequence of delegation and MCP work. The checking balance stays visible and refreshes after a human approval. A successful agent run marks the ticket resolved through the backend route, and the reset control restores the original database when a fresh run is needed.

The activity view reports agent status, findings, recommendations, and observed tool calls. It does not expose private model reasoning. Completed Boss summaries and pending invoice proposals are recovered after a page refresh from the recorded agent events plus current invoice data. Audit entries keep privacy-filtered action drafts for human review and record approved cash changes for reconciliation, while customer-message drafts and raw model reasoning remain out of the audit stream. The layout collapses to a single column on narrow screens and keeps ticket controls and approval actions keyboard accessible.
